# Sky Rescue — the bar

Critics judge the running game against this page. They look at real pixels and filmstrips, never at a builder's summary.

## Visual reference (ref-1, generated for this project)
Canva media `MAHWFe19Aus` is a 16:9 still, described here because it can't be stored in the repo.

- **Camera:** a third-person chase camera, behind and **above** the helicopter, looking slightly down (about 20–25°).
  The horizon sits in the upper third of the frame.
- **Helicopter:** chunky and toy-like, **orange-red with white trim**. It sits centred-low in the frame and takes up about
  **25–30% of the frame width**. It visibly **banks** into turns, and the rotor reads as a blurred disc.
- **Water:** saturated **turquoise**, lighter and cyan in the shallows around islands, with visible sparkle and wave
  shading. It's never a flat single colour.
- **Islands:** stylised low-poly **green** islands with sandy rims and palm trees. Several are visible at different depths.
  There's a hazy mountain silhouette on the horizon.
- **Targets that read instantly:**
  - a **golden glowing ring** straight ahead in the flight path;
  - a **fire with a tall dark smoke column** on an island to one side;
  - small boats and rafts dotted on the water.
- **Light:** warm afternoon sun, soft atmospheric fog towards the horizon, and a bright, happy palette.
- **HUD:** minimal and kept to the corners.
  - top-left: a counter pill;
  - top-right: a timer or score pill;
  - bottom-left: a virtual joystick;
  - bottom-right: a round action button.
  - The centre of the screen stays clear.

## Motion reference
The motion reference is **After Burner II** (Sega, 1987 arcade), as widely documented in longplay footage. This is from
knowledge of the game, because this environment can't play video.

- **Speed is relentless.** The world streams towards the camera on a rail, and the player never controls throttle.
  Ground and sea detail rush past fast enough to feel dangerous.
- **The roll is exaggerated.** Steering rolls the aircraft (and the camera horizon) hard, 30–60°, and it snaps back
  quickly when the stick is released.
- **The camera is loose.** It lags slightly behind the aircraft's lateral motion, so moves feel weighty, not glued.
- **Dodging is readable.** Threats appear far enough ahead to react to, about 1–1.5 s, then pass close for thrills.
- **Hits punch.** A hit brings screen shake, a flash, a short pause or slow-down, and particles. A kill or success gives a
  burst, a sound, and a score pop.

## Kid adaptation (ages 7–12, geo-kids site rules)
The game keeps the arcade verbs but swaps the violence:
- guns and missiles become a **water cannon and water bombs** on **wildfires**;
- squadrons become **rescues** and **rings**;
- obstacles are sea stacks, birds and storm clouds.

There's no game over. Running out of hearts means a friendly "try the stage again".

## "Wins" means all of these
1. A side-by-side of a game frame and this description gets a critic's verdict of *"matches the bar"* on camera, colour,
   helicopter size, readable targets and a clean HUD.
2. A filmstrip of steering shows a clear bank and roll, with a return to level.
3. A filmstrip of a hit shows shake, a flash and particles.
4. A bot and a critic both play **all three stages from start to finish**.
5. It runs at 30+ fps on a mid-range phone profile, with no console errors and a mobile layout at 375px.
