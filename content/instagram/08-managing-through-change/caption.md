The hardest part of a monolith-to-microservices split was never the architecture. It was the people who used to sit next to each other and now don't.

I've managed teams through re-orgs, layoffs, and a full service split, and the technical migration is genuinely the easy part to explain in a slide deck. What's harder is that the QA process that worked when everyone owned "the app" together stops working the moment ownership gets carved into pieces. Suddenly it's five teams each swearing their service is fine, and the bug lives in the seam between two of them that nobody quite owns.

Trust erodes fast when process changes faster than people can absorb it. My job in those stretches wasn't writing new test plans. It was making sure people still felt safe raising their hand and saying "I don't understand this new setup yet" without it looking like weakness.

geo-kids is a one-person team, which is its own kind of relief some weeks. No seams to fall between except the ones I make myself.
