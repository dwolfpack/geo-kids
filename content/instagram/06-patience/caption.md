My daughter has asked me to explain the same capital city four times this week. Not because she forgot — because she likes hearing me say it.

Somewhere around the third time I noticed the parallel to work. A junior engineer asking the same question a different way isn't wasting your time, they're still building the model in their head, one pass at a time. A flaky test that fails the same way every third run isn't being annoying on purpose, it's telling you something you haven't listened to closely enough yet.

Patience with a six-year-old is not the same muscle as patience with a test suite. But it's adjacent. Both require actually staying present instead of just waiting for your turn to talk, or for the test run to finish so you can move on.

Building geo-kids with her is slow. She wants to "help" by mashing keys sometimes. I let her. Some of my favorite parts of the game came from her breaking something I thought was finished.
