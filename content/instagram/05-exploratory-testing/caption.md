"So you're the person who clicks around and finds bugs?"

Basically, yes. And it's harder than it sounds.

Exploratory testing gets treated like the unglamorous warm-up act before "real" testing — automation, checklists, whatever comes next. I've never seen it that way. It's a skill on its own. Knowing which corner of an app to poke at, which input a developer definitely didn't think about, which combination of clicks nobody wrote a test case for because nobody imagined a real user doing that. That's judgment, built from years of watching software fail in creative ways.

Building geo-kids solo reminded me how much I rely on that instinct even outside a "real" job. There's no test plan for a side project. I just play the game the way a kid would — impatiently, out of order, tapping things twice, getting bored halfway through a level — and the bugs show up.

Manual testing isn't the step before the real work. For a lot of what actually breaks in production, it is the real work.
