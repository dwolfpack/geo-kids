I built the difficulty levels in geo-kids around population data. Then my kid proved me completely wrong.

My first version split all 195 countries into 5 levels by population — roughly 30 countries in each of the easy tiers, 5 apiece in the two hardest. Made sense on paper. Big countries first, tiny ones last.

Except she breezed through "hard" countries like Portugal (population: 10 million, ranked low on my chart) and got stuck on "easy" ones with way more people but zero cultural footprint in a first grader's world.

Population was the wrong proxy. It was measuring the wrong thing entirely. What actually predicts whether a kid recognizes a country is whether she's heard of it — from a story, a food, a flag she's seen before — not how many people live there.

So I rebuilt the tiers around recognizability instead: five much more even groups, about 39 countries each. Population is still in the data. It's just not driving difficulty anymore.

Best product lesson I've gotten from a seven-year-old.
