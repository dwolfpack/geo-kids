Try explaining sprint velocity to someone whose two best testers just got called up for miluim with four days' notice. Go on. I'll wait.

This is normal here in a way it isn't most places. You plan a quarter, and the plan has an asterisk on it that everyone understands without saying out loud: reserve duty happens, and it happens to good people at bad times.

The instinct from outside is to treat it like any other absence and just reshuffle the board. It isn't like any other absence. People come back tired in ways a vacation doesn't produce, sometimes carrying things that have nothing to do with the sprint at all.

What actually works is boring: overlap knowledge before it's needed, not after someone's already gone. If only one person understands a flaky area of the system, that's not a resourcing gap, that's a risk sitting quietly on your board with nobody's name on it.

Israeli tech has this reputation for intensity and informality, and it's earned, but the intensity isn't really about hours. It's about how much gets absorbed and normalized that would stop a team cold anywhere else.

I've shipped features the same week reserve call-ups went out across the office. Not because anyone was heroic. Because the work has to keep moving and people decide, quietly, that it will.

There's a kind of resilience you can't put in a retro template. It's just: the standup happens, someone's missing, everyone adjusts, nobody makes a thing of it, and you move forward anyway.
